# AGENTS.md — how to rebuild this repository exactly

This file is for any agent (or person) who has to reproduce, extend or audit this repository. It is written as a
recipe: section 4 states every rule, section 5 every step in order, and section 6 gives the paper's results and the
file each number and figure comes from.

## 1. What this repository is

The analysis behind the paper on the JWST galaxy papers of 2023-2026, built on one frozen snapshot of NASA ADS taken on
2026-10-19 (the freeze): which galaxy papers use JWST data (the sample, 2,227 papers), how their topics moved, how
their titles changed, and what goes with a paper being highly cited. Every result comes from the sample.

The ADS abstracts and full-text passages are not in this repository (copyright of their publishers): every field is
kept with the text emptied. Step 0 says how to reproduce the paper without them, and how to restore them.

## 2. Rules for agents

- **ADS token:** read only from the environment variable `ADS_TOKEN`. Never print it, never write it into any file,
  notebook output or commit. Get it from https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/user/settings/token.
- **ADS limits:** 5,000 ordinary queries and 100 bigqueries per day per token. Every pull is cached in
  `data/<freeze>/`; a rerun with the caches in place makes no queries.
- **The freeze:** citation counts and every other time-dependent field come only from the pulls of the freeze date.
  Never re-pull them on a later date; a change of rule must work on the cached pulls (fixed fields, such as abstracts
  or arXiv classes, may be fetched later).
- **Hand-made inputs are never regenerated by code:** `galaxy_verdicts.csv`, `catchy_title_labels.csv`, and the
  validation decisions in `validation/`.
- **ADS texts:** never commit them (see step 0).
- **Environment:** Python 3.12 with the packages pinned in `requirements.txt`. Notebooks are executed with
  `jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace` from their own folder.

## 4. The rules — every decision made about a paper

**Principles.**
1. One automatic rule per decision, applied to every paper in the same way.
2. Hand verdicts only on sets defined by a rule (every flagged paper), never on a random subset, and only where this
   section says so.
3. One review vocabulary: in / out / unsure, with a reason for out.
4. Validation measures, last, and never edits: the rule is frozen first; blind random samples are judged under one
   definition fixed beforehand; any "out" counts as an error.

**Every decision, in one form.**

| Decision | Automatic rule | Hand step | Values | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sample, step 1: candidate | ADS query `CANDIDATE_Q` | none | in / out | sample |
| Sample, step 2: uses JWST data | `bibgroup:JWST`, or one text passage scoring ≥ 7 | none | evidence: curated / explicit text / weak text / none | sample |
| Sample, step 3: article or preprint | ADS doctype | none | in / out | sample |
| Sample, step 4: galaxy paper | primary arXiv class not SR/EP; galaxy screen | verdict on every flagged paper | in / out | sample |
| Topic | one phrase list per topic (nine topics), matched locally in title + abstract | phrase lists chosen by the author | 0/1 per topic | trends, features |
| Catchy title | prefix rules, then reading | every title read | catchy / borderline / not | catchy test, feature |
| Content and data flags, first author | regexes; ORCID or full-name identity | none | features | citation model |
| Validation | none | blind review of random samples | in / out (reason) / unsure | purity, completeness only |

**The sample rule** (`common/jwst_use.py`, `general/01_sample.ipynb`). A paper is in the sample when it passes four
steps, in this order; the first step it fails is the reason it is left out (`rejected.csv`, column `status`).

1. **Candidate.** It matches the ADS candidate query (`CANDIDATE_Q`), publication year 2023 or later, collection
   astronomy:
   `abs:("galaxy" OR "galaxies") AND (arxiv_class:("astro-ph.GA" OR "astro-ph.CO") OR (*:* NOT arxiv_class:astro-ph*)) AND full:"JWST" AND (abs:"JWST" OR title:"JWST" OR full:(NIRSpec OR NIRCam OR NIRISS OR MIRI OR "JWST data" OR "JWST observations" OR "JWST imaging" OR "JWST spectroscopy" OR "JWST spectra" OR "JWST photometry" OR CEERS OR JADES OR UNCOVER OR PRIMER OR "COSMOS-Web" OR NGDEEP OR FRESCO OR GLASS))`.
   Preprint and published records of a paper are merged first.
2. **Uses JWST data.** It has one of two kinds of evidence (column `evidence`):
   - *curated*: it is in ADS's curated JWST bibliography (`bibgroup:JWST`, kept by STScI);
   - *explicit text*: one passage of its text scores **at least 7 points** (`DATA_USE_MIN_SCORE`): 7 for STScI's
     JWST data acknowledgement or a "Facilities: JWST" tag; otherwise +2 JWST data wording, +1 instrument name,
     +2 survey / programme / MAST / GO identifier, +2 "we use" or past-tense wording, +2 data claimed in the title
     or abstract, −3 prediction wording. Passages: three ADS highlight passes over the full text, plus each
     abstract sentence naming JWST, an instrument or a survey. `jwst_score` is the best passage's score.
   Papers whose best passage scores 5–6, without `bibgroup:JWST` (*weak text*), are left out.
3. **Journal article or preprint** (ADS doctype `article` or `eprint`).
4. **Galaxy paper.** Primary arXiv class not astro-ph.SR or astro-ph.EP. Then the **galaxy screen** runs on every
   paper that reaches this step: it flags a paper whose title or abstract names an unambiguous non-galaxy subject
   (`SCREEN_ALWAYS`: protostars, astrochemistry, brown dwarfs, Magellanic Clouds, Orion, Cepheids, TRGB, Galactic
   Center ...) or a likely one with no extragalactic term (`SCREEN_UNLESS_EXTRAGALACTIC` without `EXTRAGALACTIC`).
   A flagged paper is out if its verdict in `data/<freeze>/galaxy_verdicts.csv` is `not_galaxy`, in if `galaxy` or
   `unsure`; every flagged paper of the freeze has a verdict. A flagged paper with no verdict passes provisionally
   (`galaxy_check` "awaiting review", `galaxy_status` in `common/jwst_use.py`) until it is judged; that happens only
   when a rule or a text changes, and such a sample is not a reviewed one until the verdicts are given.
   **Scope:** out = stars, star formation, the ISM and astrochemistry in the Milky Way and the Local Group
   (including the Magellanic Clouds); planets, brown dwarfs, the solar system; laboratory astrochemistry; the
   stellar distance ladder; papers not about galaxies. In = everything about galaxies beyond the Local Group,
   including their resolved stars, clusters and H II regions, and galaxy-scale theory.

`galaxy_verdicts.csv` holds one row per verdict given by the author (bibcode, verdict, source, date; the latest wins).
The rule reads a verdict only for a flagged paper. No paper is added or removed by name outside these four steps.

**Trying another rule without touching the committed files.** `sample.csv` and `rejected.csv` keep, for each of the
8,959 candidates, what every step needs: `jwst_score` and `evidence` (step 2), `doctype` (step 3), `galaxy_screen` (the
terms that flag it, empty if not flagged; step 4), and `status`, the first step it failed. The arXiv classes are in
`data/<freeze>/arxiv_class.json` (the primary class first), the verdicts in `galaxy_verdicts.csv`. The data-use
threshold at 6 instead of 7, for example: of the rejected papers, those with status "weak text" and `jwst_score` 6
newly pass step 2 (342); then `common.ads.is_paper(doctype)` (164 left), primary class not in `NON_GALAXY_PRIMARY`
(157), and no `not_galaxy` verdict when flagged (149). The sample would grow from 2,227 to 2,376 provisionally: 10 of
the 149 are flagged with no verdict ("awaiting review"), so 2,366-2,376 once judged. Topics, citations and models are
not recomputed by such a count: a paper outside the sample of the freeze has no citation count in the frozen pull, and
none may be pulled now (section 2).

**The topic list** (`common/trending_shared.py`, `TOPICS`). Notebook 03 lists every two- and three-word phrase that
appears in at least six titles of the sample, ranked by the change of its share of titles from 2023-2024 to
2025-2026 (`trending_phrases_sample.csv`; "little red dots" tops it); the author picked the topics from that list and
from the main subjects of JWST galaxy studies, then wrote the phrase lists. Nine topics, each a set of phrases
(regular expressions, whole words, case-insensitive): First galaxies (the phrases, or a redshift of 10 or more),
Little Red Dots, Black holes & AGN (without the Little Red Dot papers), Population III, Reionization, Quenching,
Dust, Chemical abundances, Lensing. They are matched **locally** in the title and abstract of each sample paper, on
texts fetched once (`topic_texts.json`: title, abstract, publication date, all fixed fields), so no ADS query
depends on the topic list. The same nine topics in every table, figure and model feature.
**Direction of a topic** (Extended Data Table 2, `checks/topic_sensitivity.py`): gaining when its share of papers in
2026 is more than 20% above its 2023 value (relative change, e.g. 10% to more than 12%), losing when more than 20%
below, steady otherwise.

**Catchy titles** (`data/<freeze>/catchy_title_labels.csv`, labels catchy / borderline / not): in every result a
title is catchy or not; borderline counts as not catchy.

**Validation** (`general/tools/make_validation_review.py`, `score_validation.py`): one question per paper, does it
belong in the sample? In = a galaxy paper that uses JWST data (observations, reduction, analysis, catalogues)
or, if it does not, shows JWST data in its figures (as labelled in their captions), whatever the kind of paper.
Verdicts: in (analyses JWST data / JWST data in figures), out (no JWST data / not a galaxy paper), unsure.

## 5. Recipe

### Step 0 — environment, and the two ways to reproduce
```
python3.12 -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate    # Python 3.12: a newer Python may not install the pins
python -m pip install -r requirements.txt
```
- **From the frozen tables, no token:** `./run_paper.sh` runs steps 5-12 and 14 on the committed tables and caches,
  then `checks/verify_outputs.py`, which fails unless every output holds what the committed one holds: the same
  pixels in each figure and the same numbers to 0.001 in each table. The bytes of a PNG can change with the version
  of the image libraries, and the last digit of a rounded number with the platform, so `git status` can list changed
  files while the check passes.
- **Everything, from the ADS pulls on:** the ADS abstracts and full-text passages are emptied (null) in this
  repository, and `data/<freeze>/texts_removed.json` marks it; every step that reads them stops
  (`common.config.require_texts`). With a token, `python general/tools/restore_texts.py` fetches them again with the
  parameters of the step that first pulled them, writes them back in the original order and format, compares each
  file with `manifest.json` and removes the marker; then `./run_all.sh` (step 16). Every text lookup of the
  repository asks ADS by `identifier` (`common.ads.highlights_for`, `by_request`), because ADS now files many
  preprints of the freeze under their published bibcode, and stores each text under the bibcode that was asked for.
  The caches of the freeze were filled by `bibcode`, when the two gave the same records.
  ADS serves its current version of each text, and some passages it no longer returns at all, so the restored files
  differ from the frozen ones and a later rerun does not give exactly the committed results: a few papers enter or
  leave the sample on today's text (a rerun on 2026-11-11 gave 2,246 papers). The citation snapshot stays the one of
  the freeze (notebooks 03 and 04 pull no citation data after the freeze date): a paper that enters gets no citation
  count and is left out of the models. Numbers near a threshold can move, such as the number of effects that survive
  the false-discovery correction (19 instead of 21 in that rerun). The committed outputs are the reference.
```
export ADS_TOKEN=...                      # never committed
```

### Step 1 — the data folder of the freeze (`data/2026-10-19/`)
ADS pulls of the freeze date: the candidate search with its first highlight pass (`raw_candidate_search.json`), the
citations of every sample paper (`topics_sample_pull.json`, with ADS's own phrase search per topic, used by the
topic-matcher check), the citing papers and their authors (`features_citing_authors.json`), their ORCIDs
(`citation_orcids.json`) and dates (`citing_dates.json`). Per-paper caches of fixed fields: `doctypes.json`,
`abstracts.json`, `arxiv_class.json`, `jwst_use_highlights.json`, `jwst_deep_highlights.json`, `topic_texts.json`,
`features_ads_metadata.json`, `features_programme_snippets.json`, `features_author_identity.json`. Hand inputs:
`galaxy_verdicts.csv` (with the decision files of the screen pages), `catchy_title_labels.csv` (with the edits file
of the review page), `validation/` (key files, decisions, review caches). `manifest.json`: checksum, size and
records of every file.

### How to run a notebook
From the notebook's own folder:
```
jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace --ExecutePreprocessor.timeout=-1 <notebook>.ipynb
```
Notebooks put the repository root on `sys.path` themselves (`sys.path.insert(0, "..")`).

### Step 2 — the sample: `general/01_sample.ipynb`
Candidate search with first-pass highlights, duplicate merging, per-paper lookups (document type, abstract, arXiv
class, second and third highlight passes; all cached), then the four steps of section 4. Writes
`general/outputs/sample.csv` (columns `evidence`, `jwst_score`, `galaxy_screen` terms, `galaxy_check`, and every
identifier of each paper), `rejected.csv` (with `status`), `sample.html`, `rejected.html`.
Result: 9,196 candidate records → 8,959 papers after merging → **2,227 in the sample** (1,851 curated, 376 explicit
text). Left out: 5,083 no data-use evidence, 853 weak text, 417 not articles or preprints, 379 not galaxy papers (158
arXiv class, 221 screen verdicts).

**Hand step, galaxy screen:** `python general/tools/galaxy_screen.py page` writes `general/outputs/galaxy_screen.html`
with every sample paper the screen flags that has no verdict yet; the author saves
`galaxy_screen_decisions_<date>.json` into `data/<freeze>/`; `python general/tools/galaxy_screen.py merge` adds the
verdicts to `galaxy_verdicts.csv` (never overwrite a decisions file); then rerun from step 2.

### Step 3 — topics and titles: `general/03_topics_sample_titles.ipynb`
Phrase discovery on the sample's titles; the citations of every sample paper through one ADS bigquery
(`topics_sample_pull.json`, the citation snapshot of steps 4-11); the topic list matched locally; share of papers
and of citations per topic and year; catchy titles (labels joined through any identifier of the paper; year-matched
bootstrap of the median citations). Writes `general/outputs/sample_table.csv` (one row per sample paper: citations,
evidence, the nine topic flags, title label), `trending_phrases_sample.csv`, `general/plots/topic_attention_sample*`.
Result: 186 catchy and 39 borderline titles (of 2,226 labelled); catchy titles 3.7% (2023) → 10.5% (2026); catchy
vs. other titles +4 median citations [−1.5, +13], P = 0.87 (not significant).
**Hand step, catchy labels:** every new title of a freeze is labelled before this step (rules in the notebook,
section 5). `python general/tools/make_catchy_review.py` writes `general/outputs/catchy_titles_review.html`; "Save
labels" downloads `catchy_title_labels_edits_<date>.json`, which is merged into the CSV; then rerun from step 3.

### Step 4 — citation model: `general/04_citation_features.ipynb`
Features from ADS metadata, programme snippets and first-author histories (caches in `data/<freeze>/`), the
within-month citation percentile, ridge associations, boosted-tree predictions, robustness tests 1-10. Writes
`general/outputs/citation_features.csv`, `first_author_records.csv` (the first author's identity, rule, ADS query,
earlier papers and career start for every paper), `general/plots/associations.csv`, `robustness_summary.csv`,
`robustness_tree_grid.csv`, `top_cited_prediction_check.*`.
**Target:** citation percentile (0-100, ties averaged) among the papers posted on arXiv in the same month, months
with fewer than 25 papers pooled with the next (`MIN_COHORT`); papers posted less than three months before the
citation date are left out (`MIN_AGE_MONTHS = 3`: those from July 2026 on, 172 of the 2,227). **Features** (58,
groups in the notebook's `GROUPS`): timing (topic_rank_earliest, log_topic_papers_before), data (n_programmes,
newest_cycle, data_age_years, public_survey_data, gto_data, abs_nircam, abs_nirspec, abs_miri, abs_niriss, abs_prism,
abs_ifu, abs_grism, abs_alma, abs_hst), team (log_n_authors, large_collab, n_countries, n_regions, fa_europe,
fa_n_america, fa_asia, fa_log_prior_papers, fa_career_years, fa_shared_name), content (the nine topic flags,
catalogue_or_release, instrument_paper, abs_claims, abs_very_high_z, abs_hedging = count of may / might / suggest* /
tentative / possibl* / candidate in the abstract, abs_log_words, abs_numbers_per_100w), form (refereed,
journal_nature_science, journal_apjl, journal_apj, journal_aa, journal_mnras, journal_other, letter, log_pages,
log_references, open_data_code, title_words, title_separator, title_question, title_survey_name, title_catchy). Logs
are log10(1 + n) except log_n_authors and log_pages (log10 n); first-author counts stop at the year before
publication. **Models:** ridge = median imputation with a 0/1 indicator per imputed column, standardisation,
`RidgeCV(alphas=np.logspace(-1, 4, 30))`; trees = `HistGradientBoostingRegressor(max_iter=300, learning_rate=0.04,
max_leaf_nodes=15, min_samples_leaf=30, l2_regularization=1.0, random_state=0)`, missing values handled natively.
**Split:** trained once on the 1,212 papers posted before 2025-04 (`TEST_FROM`), tested on the 843 posted 2025-04 to
2026-06. **Associations:** ridge on all 2,055 papers with the journal block coded against ApJ (`refereed` and
`journal_apj` replaced by `preprint` and `journal_apjs`), median imputation without indicators, standardised,
penalty chosen by cross-validation. **Robustness tests** (each changes one thing and refits): 1 tree settings
(225), 2 number of trees and settings chosen on a validation period, 3 chance (test and training papers
resampled), 4 random forests, 5 null test (citation ranks shuffled), 6 information at posting time, 7 data quality,
8 target and design, 9 the ridge associations (penalty, elastic net, bootstrap signs, variance inflation, the two
first-author features), 10 self-citations (citations from other authors only; each ridge fit chooses its penalty on
its own target).
Result: 2,055 papers modelled; test ρ = 0.541 (trees) / 0.540 (ridge), AUC 0.80; 5-fold cross-validation by month 0.57.
**Hand step, first-author records:** `python general/tools/author_check.py page` writes
`general/outputs/author_check.html`: 100 people drawn at random (seed 20261019) from the 1,234 first authors of the
modelled papers, each with the record the model uses (rule, query, spellings counted, papers per year, links to the
ADS search and the ORCID record). Verdict per person: right record, wrong record (another person's papers counted),
unsure. "Save decisions" downloads `author_check_decisions_<date>.json` into `data/<freeze>/`;
`python general/tools/author_check.py score` gives the share of wrong records with its exact 95% interval
(`general/outputs/author_check.csv`). No ADS query and no ADS text.

### Step 5 — rank stability: `python general/05_rank_stability.py` (needs step 4)
Dates every citing paper (arXiv posting month, else publication month; `citing_dates.json`), rebuilds the
within-month percentile of papers at least 24 months old as it stood 6 and 12 months after posting, and compares it
with the rank at the snapshot date; then the trees of step 4, trained on the 6-month rank with whole months held out,
scored against the rank at the snapshot date. Writes `general/outputs/rank_stability.csv`,
`general/plots/rank_stability_summary.csv`, `rank_stability.png`.
Result: 975 papers posted 2022-07 to 2024-10. Rank at 6 months vs. the snapshot date ρ = 0.89 (74% of the 6-month top
10% still top 10%), at 12 months ρ = 0.95 (83%). The model trained on the 6-month rank predicts the rank at the
snapshot date with ρ = 0.57, the model trained on that rank itself with 0.56 (and the 6-month rank with 0.54).

### Step 6 — curated papers only: `python general/06_sample_sensitivity.py` (needs step 4)
Refits the models of step 4 on the curated papers only, and on 50 random subsets of the same size (percentiles
recomputed within cohorts). Writes `general/outputs/sample_sensitivity_models.csv`, `sample_sensitivity_effects.csv`.
Result: curated papers only (1,836 modelled): test ρ 0.57 (random subsets of the same size 0.52-0.56); no
significant effect changes sign.

### Step 7 — baselines: `python general/07_baselines.py` (needs steps 4 and 5)
The models of step 4 refitted on small feature sets (journal, team size, topics, first author's record) on the same
time split; test ρ, AUC, and the share of the model's top 10% that is in the actual top 10% ("times chance"); next
to the 6-month citation rank of step 5. Writes `general/outputs/baselines.csv`.
Result: all 58 features ρ 0.54; 52% of the model's top 10% (44 of 84 test papers) are in the actual top 10% (4.9
times chance, the actual top 10% being 90 of the 843 test papers), so it finds 44 of the 90 (49%); team size +
first author + topics ρ 0.51; topics alone 0.35; journal alone −0.01; the 6-month citation rank 0.89.

### Step 8 — effects and false-discovery rate: `python general/08_effects.py` (needs step 4)
The ridge associations of step 4 with 2,000 bootstrap refits: 95% intervals, two-sided bootstrap p-values,
Benjamini-Hochberg correction (q < 0.05); yes/no effects as the gap in places out of 100 (effect / sqrt(p(1-p))).
**Per natural unit** (columns `unit`, `effect per unit`, `unit low/high`; `UNITS` in the script): features stored as
log10 per doubling (effect per sd × log10(2) / sd), n_programmes per programme, n_countries per country, n_regions per
continent, abs_claims per claim, fa_career_years per 10 years, title_words per 5 words; yes/no features with vs.
without. Writes `general/outputs/effects_table.csv` and `general/plots/effects_fdr.png` (why the correction, and
where the Benjamini-Hochberg line cuts the 58 p-values) and `general/plots/effects_vif.png` (the variance inflation
factor of every feature, VIF = 1 / (1 - R²) of the feature regressed on the other 57: median 1.4, largest ApJ Letters
5.7 and the letter format 5.4, which overlap by construction).
Result: 20 of 58 effects survive (title length, q = 0.052, just misses); Little Red Dots +27 places [23, 32], Nature/Science +25 [20, 31], NIRSpec in the
abstract +7, very high redshift +6, chemical abundances +5, IFU −6, HST −4, ALMA −4; references +5.2 per doubling,
first author's earlier papers +2.3 per doubling, authors +2.2 per doubling, countries +0.9 per country, career −1.6
per 10 years.
**First-author records, hand check** (step 4, `general/tools/author_check.py`): round 1 found 18 of 95 judged records
wrong (19% [12, 28]) under the first rules: stray old papers of namesakes counted, and gaps under 10 years left in the
career; the rules then changed (gap of 7 years, nothing counted before the career start; on the round-1 verdicts
this removes the strays in 11 of the 18 and changes no right record by more than three papers). Round 2, 100 other
people under the final rules: 11 of 98 judged records wrong (11% [6, 19]), mostly a namesake with the same full name
in another field or an early paper under a similar name (`general/outputs/author_check_r2.csv`).

### Step 9 — self-citations: `python general/09_self_citations.py` (needs step 4)
The citations of test 10 (`features_citing_authors.json`) matched author by author three ways: A. surname + first
initial; B. ORCID when both authors have one, else A; C. ORCID, else surname + full first name (`citation_orcids.json`:
orcid_pub, orcid_user, orcid_other). Writes `general/outputs/self_citations.csv`, `self_citations_effects.csv`.
Result: 66% of citing-author positions have an ORCID; self-citation share 41.7% (A), 41.6% (B), 41.4% (C); 22% for
teams of 1-5 authors, 53% above 50; without self-citations test ρ 0.54 → 0.47 (ridge; 0.46 trees), and the team-size effect +2.2 → −0.15
[−1.11, +0.84] per doubling of the team (rule B).

### Step 10 — prediction check: `python general/10_prediction_check.py` (needs step 4)
The boosted trees of step 4 scored three ways on papers they never saw: the time split; five-fold cross-validation
holding out whole posting months; each quarter from 2024 on predicted from earlier papers (at least 40 papers, 95%
intervals from 500 bootstrap resamples). Writes `general/outputs/prediction_check.csv` (one row per modelled paper)
and `prediction_forward.csv` (one row per quarter).
Result: test ρ 0.541 [0.486, 0.593], AUC 0.805, the top 20% of predictions hold 66% of the top-10% papers;
cross-validation ρ 0.570 ± 0.038; quarters ρ 0.44-0.66.

### Step 11 — topics by half-year: `python general/11_sample_topics_by_half_year.py` (needs step 3)
Per topic and half-year (publication month, from 2023 H1; the freeze date's half-year is partial): number of sample
papers on the topic and share of the half-year's sample papers. Writes `general/outputs/sample_topics_by_half_year.csv`.
Result: 135 papers in 2023 H1, 467 in 2026 H1; first galaxies 31 → 59 papers (23% → 13%), Little Red Dots 0 → 59
(0 → 13%), reionization 28 → 59 (21% → 13%).

### Step 12 — validation: `python general/tools/score_validation.py`
Three rounds of blind random samples (`python general/tools/make_validation_review.py` writes the review page
`general/outputs/validation.html`, papers P001-P400, and the key files `data/<freeze>/validation/round<k>_key.csv`:
stratum and weight, not to be opened before the review is finished; caches `validation/review_highlights.json`,
`review_metadata.json`). Round 1 (seed 20261020): 75 accepted papers, 45 rejected with score 3-4, 30 with score
0-2; round 1 was judged twice, blind, to measure the rater's consistency. Round 2 (seed 20261027): 60 / 25 / 65,
excluding round 1. Round 3 (seed 20261029): 75 sample papers and 25 weak-text papers, excluding rounds 1-2.
`score_validation.py` places every reviewed paper where the rule puts it (accepted, weak text, score 3-4, score 0-2:
the rejected cells hold the articles and preprints rejected at the data-use step whose primary arXiv class is not
SR/EP, N = 359, 765, 2,597), takes the share of "in" papers per cell, and weights by the pool sizes. Purity = share
in among accepted papers (all rounds, 184 papers); completeness = N_acc p_acc / (N_acc p_acc + Σ N_c p_c) over the
rejected cells (all rounds). 4,000 bootstrap resamples within cells; unsure papers bound the estimate. Writes
`general/outputs/validation_summary.csv`, `validation_cells.csv`, `validation_agreement.csv`.
Result: purity 94.6% [91, 98] (174 of 184 sample papers belong), completeness 64% [59, 69]; agreement between the
two passes over round 1: 93%, κ = 0.84.

### Step 13 — checks
- `python checks/topic_search.py`: ADS's own phrase search per topic, with the phrase lists of the matcher written as
  ADS phrases (`ADS_PHRASES`, checked against `TOPICS` before any query; element ratios and the First-galaxies
  redshift pattern cannot be searched), among the sample papers: title, abstract and keywords, fixed fields. Writes
  `data/<freeze>/topics_sample_search.json`; a rerun with the same queries makes no query. Edit `ADS_PHRASES` with
  `TOPICS`. (The search inside `topics_sample_pull.json` used the lists of the freeze date, before phrases were added
  to three of them.)
- `python checks/topic_matcher.py`: the local topic matcher against that search, on the 2,227 sample papers: papers
  found by both, by ADS only (a phrase in the keywords only) and by the matcher only, and the Jaccard index per topic
  (`checks/topic_matcher.csv`; `checks/topic_matcher.png` shows what the index measures and the overlap per topic).
  Result (search of 2026-11-16): 0.85-1.00 for seven topics (median 0.97); the differences are papers that name the
  phrase only in their keywords, plus 10 element-ratio papers found by the matcher only. Population III 0.46: the
  MNRAS / A&A keyword "dark ages, reionization, first stars" adds 70 reionization papers that do not name the topic.
- `python checks/first_galaxies_threshold.py`: the First galaxies share by year for a redshift threshold of 8, 9 or
  10 (`checks/first_galaxies_threshold.csv`): it falls by 34-37% from 2023 to 2026 whatever the threshold, while the
  papers found by the phrases alone stay between 4% and 6%.
- `python checks/topic_sensitivity.py`: the topic trends with narrow, current and broad phrase lists
  (`checks/topic_sensitivity.csv`, Extended Data Table 2).
- `python checks/manifest.py`: writes `data/<freeze>/manifest.json` (checksum, size, records, pull time of every data
  file).
- `python checks/catchy_llm_labels.py`: the catchy-title results with the labels of a large language model
  (`data/<freeze>/catchy_title_labels_llm.csv`, every sample title labelled with the written rules; 94.8% agreement
  with the author's, 91 catchy instead of 186): share 2.7% (2023) → 4.8% (2026), effect p = 0.38, test ρ 0.54
  (`checks/catchy_llm_labels.csv`; with the author's labels it reproduces the effect table and ρ 0.541).
- `python checks/verify_outputs.py`: compares the rebuilt outputs with the committed ones (same pixels, same numbers
  to 0.001); the last step of `run_paper.sh`.

### Step 16 — everything at once: `ADS_TOKEN=... ./run_all.sh`
Steps 2-15 in order; stops at the first error. Run on texts restored at a later date, it makes a few ADS queries for
texts that are not cached (fixed fields only) and its outputs differ slightly from the committed ones (step 0);
`python checks/verify_outputs.py` then lists them. Everything computed over the sample follows it: the topic and
catchy-title shares, the modelled papers, and the validation estimates, whose pool is the sample (purity and
completeness can move slightly). The citation snapshot, the hand inputs, the rules and the model settings do not.

## 6. The paper: its results, and the file each comes from

**What it finds:**
1. The sample: one automatic rule, 8,959 candidates -> 2,227 "JWST galaxy papers" (1,851 curated, 376 by text);
   purity about 95%, completeness about two thirds (theory papers showing JWST data in figures are the misses).
2. Topics, in the sample. Attention = papers on the topic in a period / all JWST galaxy papers of the period;
   citation share alike. 2023 -> 2026: Little Red Dots 0 -> 13% of papers and 28% of the citations to 2026 papers;
   first galaxies 20 -> 13%; reionization 20 -> 13%; black holes & AGN 26 -> 33%; quenching 8 -> 14%; lensing
   18 -> 14%; Population III 4 -> 2%; dust and chemical abundances steady near a quarter. Counts by complete
   half-years (Fig. 1b): first galaxies 31 -> 59 papers (2023 H1 -> 2026 H1) while the sample grew 135 -> 467. The
   fall of the first galaxies comes from papers naming z >= 10 (holds at 8, 9, 10); phrase-only papers steady.
3. Prediction: rho = 0.54 on the 843 test papers, calibrated; the model's top 10% catches half of the actual top 10%
   (five times chance), its top 20% two thirds; holds with months held out (0.57 +- 0.04) and quarter by
   quarter (0.44-0.66); vanishes with shuffled ranks. Topic + team size + first author 0.51; topics 0.35; journal
   -0.01. Six-month citations: rho 0.89 with the rank at the snapshot date; three in four of the 6-month top 10% are
   still there two or more years later; a model trained on the 6-month rank predicts the later rank as well (0.57 vs
   0.56).
4. Effects (ridge, false-discovery rate): Little Red Dots +27 places out of 100, Nature/Science +25, NIRSpec / very
   high redshift +6-7, chemical abundances +5; references +5 per doubling; career -2 per 10 years at fixed earlier
   papers; 20 of 58 effects survive. The team-size advantage is all self-citation (+2.2 -> -0.2 per doubling); 41% of citations are
   self-citations (22% -> 53% with team size). Catchy titles 3.7% -> 10.5%, not cited significantly more; being
   early on a topic not significant.
